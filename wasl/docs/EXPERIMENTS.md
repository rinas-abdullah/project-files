## Findings (read with the confidence intervals)

* **On-site waiting and yard congestion:** Full WASL has the lowest mean on-site wait, P95 wait and average yard queue in all seven scenarios (e.g. Peak arrivals: on-site wait ≈ 9 min vs ≈ 82 min for FCFS; average yard queue ≈ 0.6 vs ≈ 5.4 trucks). With only 3 replications many intervals are wide; the queue and P95 differences in the peak and mixed-disruption scenarios are the clearest.
* **Total delay (natural arrival → service start):** Full WASL is **not** better than FCFS. FCFS has the lower mean in every scenario (most differences are within the CIs). WASL moves waiting off-site rather than removing it, and it pays a real cost for travel latency after a call and for reserving the inspector early.
* **Throughput / makespan and utilisation:** comparable between FCFS and Full WASL; WASL is not consistently better.
* **Rigid baselines:** Fixed appointments and OR-only (plan once, no re-planning, no readiness gating) do worse than FCFS on most metrics in this model, especially under disruption. This supports re-planning on disruption as part of the decision loop.
* **Interpretation:** in this synthetic model WASL's measurable benefit is controlling yard congestion and on-site waiting (safety, gate and yard space, driver experience), not shortening total delay. These are results on synthetic data and do not demonstrate performance in a real warehouse. Raw per-replication metrics: [`experiments.raw.json`](experiments.raw.json).
